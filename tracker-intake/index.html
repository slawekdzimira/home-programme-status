Design proposal - DMS Tracker v2

One front door for everything HOME asks us to build

How the existing DMS Tracker grows from an issue log into the single shared system where HOME and the delivery team record every request, from a broken screen to a new module, agree its priority, and see when it ships.

Status Draft for discussion Date 23 September 2026 Builds on tracker-prod.home.kruko.co, main @ 69baf83 Jump to the step-by-step mockups

The problem

Requests reach the team through calls, Teams chats and e-mail, usually as a sentence with no example record, no expected outcome and no deadline. Weeks later HOME asks for a status on something that was never written down in one place, or was written down differently by two people. Nobody can answer "what are we working on for HOME, in what order, and when does it land" from a single screen.

The current tracker already solves this for one slice: DMS defects reported by HOME staff. It has configurable modules, problem types, priorities and statuses, a P0 sign-off, default assignees per category, internal notes, attachments, change history and a link to the Azure DevOps work item. What it lacks is a way to hold anything larger than a single ticket, a plan for when things ship, and a rule that makes it the only place requests live.

Goal. If HOME wants it, it is in the tracker. Every record shows who holds it now, what happens next, its priority, and the month it is planned for. Status questions are answered by a link, not by a meeting.

What we borrow from ServiceNow and its peers

Vendor-client delivery is a solved problem in enterprise service management. ServiceNow splits it across ITSM (incidents, requests, problems, changes), Strategic Portfolio Management (demands, projects, roadmaps) and Customer Service Management (the client-facing portal). Jira Service Management and Azure DevOps cover the same ground with different names. We take the concepts that fit a team of our size and leave the heavy machinery.

Operating principles

One front door
A request that is not in the tracker does not exist. Anything raised on a call or in chat is logged there by whoever heard it, within one business day.
Every record has a holder
Each status belongs to Vendor, HOME or Third party (Yardi, BASC, middleware). The list is sortable by who owes the next move.
HOME owns the order, we own the effort
HOME's product owner ranks demands and approves priorities. The delivery team sizes the work and says what fits in a release. Neither side sets the other's number.
Months, not dates
Plans show a target release month. A specific date appears only once an item is in a frozen release.
Tracker for HOME, ADO for engineering
HOME never needs an Azure DevOps login. Engineers never re-type what HOME wrote. The link between them is kept by the system, not by hand.
Nothing silently disappears
Rejected, deferred and duplicate are visible outcomes with a reason, not deletions.

Record types

The single Ticket today mixes things that behave very differently: a login failure needs to be fixed today, a new report needs a quote and a slot in the plan. Classifying by intent at intake is what lets the rest of the system (fields, workflow, SLA, approval) behave correctly.

Reclassification is normal and recorded: a "Change request" sized at 20 days is converted to a Demand; a Demand approved for delivery spawns a Project and links back to it. An Incident whose root cause needs a redesign spawns a Change request or Demand and closes with a workaround.

Hierarchy and the Azure DevOps link

A Project owns the smaller records that belong to it, which is what makes "how far along is the landlord portal" answerable. Each tracker record links to exactly one ADO item at the level that matches it, and ADO keeps its own Epic, Feature, Story, Task hierarchy underneath.

Two rules keep the two systems honest. First, the tracker record carries HOME's words and ADO carries the team's restatement; neither overwrites the other (the current sync already posts the ADO description as an internal comment rather than replacing the report). Second, progress shown to HOME is rolled up from ADO child states, so nobody maintains a percentage by hand.

Lifecycle

One shared set of stages, each type using the subset it needs. Statuses stay editable lookups as they are today; each one is mapped to a fixed stage so that SLAs, the roadmap and reports can rely on it even when a status is renamed.

TypePath through the stages
INCNew, Triage, In progress, Released (fix deployed or workaround given), Closed. HOME confirms, or the record closes itself 5 business days after release.
REQNew, In progress, Closed. Approval only where the catalogue says so (for example production data changes).
CHGNew, Triage, Sizing, Approval, Scheduled, In progress, UAT, Released, Closed.
DEMNew, Triage, Sizing (rough order of magnitude), Approval (go / defer / reject), then converted to a Project.
PRJScheduled, In progress, UAT, Released, Closed. Its own progress is the roll-up of its children.

Changes, demands and projects: one ranked list

Priority labels do not order planned work; everything ends up "high". Instead HOME's product owner keeps one ranked backlog per product, drag and drop, with a light score to support the conversation rather than replace it.

FactorScaleSet by
Business value (families served, staff time saved)1 to 5HOME

The tracker shows value plus time pressure plus risk, divided by effort, as a suggested order. HOME's rank is what counts. Changing the rank of anything already in a frozen release requires a swap: something of equal size comes out.

Approval gates

  • Priority P0 - HOME approver, as today.
  • Estimate - any CHG sized above an agreed threshold, and every Demand, waits in Approval until HOME accepts the estimate. This is where maintenance hours and change-request budget are consumed knowingly rather than discovered on the invoice.
  • UAT sign-off - a CHG or PRJ is released only after HOME passes UAT, or after an agreed silent-acceptance period.

Schedule and releases

ServiceNow's change calendar is overkill, but the idea behind it is exactly what HOME is missing: a published plan of what changes when.

  • Release becomes a record: product, name (for example DMS 2026.11), target month, freeze date, deploy window, state (Planned, Frozen, In UAT, Deployed). It maps to the deploy tags we already cut.
  • Every approved CHG and PRJ milestone targets a release. Incidents and requests are fixed as hotfixes and are attached to the release that actually carried them, so release notes build themselves.
  • Freeze: two weeks before the deploy window the release content is locked. After the freeze, additions go through the swap rule or wait for the next release.
  • Roadmap view for HOME: columns Now (current release), Next (the following one or two) and Later (ranked, not yet scheduled). Months, never days, until frozen.
  • Slipping an item to a later release requires a reason and notifies the requester; the roadmap history shows every move.

Service levels

Clocks run on business hours in El Paso time and pause in any HOME-held or third-party stage. The existing Priority.sla_hours field becomes two targets per priority. Figures below are a starting proposal to agree with HOME.

An SLA at 75 percent of its target alerts the assignee and the delivery lead; a breach is listed in the weekly triage and in the monthly report. Reporting counts breaches honestly; the pause rule is what keeps it fair to both sides.

Requests raised on calls and in chat

Calls and Teams will not go away, and they should not have to. The rule is that they feed the tracker instead of replacing it.

  1. Whoever hears the request creates the record the same day with Source set to Call, Teams, E-mail or Meeting, and Requested by set to the HOME person (the existing "Reported by" field covers people without an account).
  2. The record is created in Needs info from HOME if anything required is missing, so the requester gets one message listing exactly what is needed.
  3. After a working session, the meeting owner turns the notes into records and posts one summary link in the chat: "Logged from today's call: DEM-0142, CHG-0310, INC-0877". The link replaces the recap message.
  4. When someone asks for a status in chat, the answer is the record link. If there is no record, the answer is to create one now.
  5. Later, e-mail-in (a mailbox that creates records) and a Teams message action ("Create tracker record from this message") make step 1 a click. Both are phase 3, not a precondition.

    Data model changes

    The current code already does the hard parts (history, approval rules in save(), ADO read, attachments, SSO). The change is additive; nothing existing is rewritten.

    class RecordType(LookupModel):        # INC, REQ, CHG, DEM, PRJ
    prefix = CharField()               # reference prefix, e.g. "CHG"
    required_fields = JSONField()      # Definition of Ready for this type
    allowed_stages = JSONField()

class Stage(TextChoices):              # fixed, code relies on it
    NEW, TRIAGE, SIZING, APPROVAL, SCHEDULED,
    IN_PROGRESS, UAT, RELEASED, CLOSED, ON_HOLD, REJECTED

class Status(LookupModel):             # existing, editable
    stage = CharField(choices=Stage)   # new: what the status means
    holder = CharField(choices=[vendor, client, third_party])  # new
    pauses_sla = BooleanField()        # new

class Product(LookupModel):            # DMS, Home ERP, Lobby, Middleware...
class Module(LookupModel):             # existing, gains product FK

class Release(Model):
    product, name, target_month, freeze_date, deploy_window, state

class Ticket(Model):                   # existing, keeps T-NNNNN for old rows
    record_type = FK(RecordType)
    parent = FK("self", null=True)     # CHG/INC under a PRJ
    source = CharField(call/teams/email/meeting/portal)
    impact, urgency = CharField()      # priority derived for INC
    rank = PositiveIntegerField()      # HOME's order, per product
    value, time_pressure, risk = SmallInt()
    size = CharField(S/M/L/XL); estimate_days = Decimal()
    estimate_state = Approval          # reuses the existing enum
    needed_by = DateField()            # month precision
    target_release = FK(Release)
    sponsor = FK(User)
    ado_links = M2M(AdoLink)           # replaces the single Task URL

class SlaClock(Model):                 # one row per target per ticket
    ticket, target, started_at, paused_seconds, breached_at

    Migrations follow the repository's existing pattern (guarded defaults for rolling deploys). Existing tickets are backfilled as INC or REQ by problem type, and their statuses are mapped to stages once in a data migration.

    Rollout

    Phase 1 - One front door

    About 3 to 4 weeks of one engineer

    • Record types, stages and holders on statuses; Product lookup; parent link.
    • Definition of Ready per type in the wizard and the classic form; Needs info loop with auto-close.
    • "Waiting on HOME" and "My requests" views; Source field for call and chat intake.
    • Backfill existing tickets. Agree the intake rule with HOME and start using it.

    Phase 2 - Plan and priority

    About 3 to 4 weeks

    • Impact x urgency matrix; ranked backlog per product; estimate approval gate.
    • Release records, target release, freeze and swap rule; Roadmap view.
    • Project page with roll-up from ADO child states; multiple ADO links per record.

    Phase 3 - Service levels and automation

    About 2 to 3 weeks

    • SLA clocks with pause rules, at-risk alerts, monthly service review report.
    • E-mail-in and a Teams message action for intake.
    • Optional: create the ADO item from an approved record, so engineers never re-type.

    Phase 1 alone fixes the everyday pain: every request is written down with enough detail, and everyone can see who holds it. Phases 2 and 3 add the plan and the measurement.

    Open decisions

    1. Scope of products. Does the tracker cover DMS only, or also Home ERP / HCV, the lobby kiosk and middleware? The design assumes all of them under one Product lookup, with HOME seeing only the products they are on.
    2. HOME product owner. One named person per product who ranks and approves. Without this role the ranked backlog does not work.
    3. Estimate threshold. Above how many person-days does a change request need HOME's approval of the estimate before work starts?
    4. Release cadence. Monthly per product, or one shared monthly release train?
    5. SLA figures. The table above is a proposal; the final numbers belong in the contract, not only in the tool.
    6. Build or buy. Jira Service Management or ServiceNow CSM would cover most of this out of the box, at per-seat cost and with a second login for HOME staff. Extending the tracker keeps DMS SSO, the TCode-aware wizard and the ADO link we already have. The recommendation is to extend.
